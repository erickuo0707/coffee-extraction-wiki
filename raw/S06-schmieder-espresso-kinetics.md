# S06 — Flow, particle size, temperature, and espresso kinetics

- **Citation:** Schmieder, B.K.L., Pannusch, V.B., Vannieuwenhuyse, L., Briesen, H., & Minceva, M. (2023), “Influence of Flow Rate, Particle Size, and Temperature on Espresso Extraction Kinetics,” *Foods*, 12(15), 2871.
- **DOI / full text:** https://doi.org/10.3390/foods12152871 · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10418593/
- **Source type / license:** Peer-reviewed primary research; open access under Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 (CC BY 4.0).
- **Access checked:** 2026-11-15. PMC article page, methods, results, conclusions, DOI, and license checked.
- **Scope:** A controlled espresso study collecting ten consecutive fractions and measuring TDS and selected compounds. It varied flow rate (1–3 mL/s), grinder settings, and brew temperature (80–98 °C), with a fixed coffee dose and a particular Colombian Arabica coffee / espresso setup.
- **Ingested takeaways (paraphrased):** Flow rate had the strongest of the studied operating-parameter effects on the measured component masses; its influence varied with grind setting and temperature. The study also found that small recipe differences in beverage mass/brew ratio could matter more for consistency than the smaller process-parameter changes tested.
- **Limits:** Results are specific to this espresso setup, bean, tested parameter range, and measured compounds. The paper did not conduct a sensory preference test. Its measurements do not support a universal flavor prediction from flow rate or temperature.
- **Use in this Wiki:** Adds primary evidence about espresso extraction kinetics and interactions among variables; contrasts with drip-brew sensory studies without treating the results as interchangeable.
