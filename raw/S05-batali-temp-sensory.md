# S05 — Brew temperature and drip-coffee sensory profile

- **Citation:** Batali, M.E., Ristenpart, W.D., & Guinard, J.-X. (2020), “Brew temperature, at fixed brew strength and extraction, has little impact on the sensory profile of drip brew coffee,” *Scientific Reports*, 10, 16450.
- **DOI / PubMed record:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-020-73341-4 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33020560/
- **Source type:** Peer-reviewed primary sensory study; open-access article record with an author correction.
- **Access checked:** 2026-11-15. PubMed abstract and the PMC author correction were checked. The PMC full-text page for the primary article presented a browser challenge in this session; its full text was not reviewed here.
- **Tested conditions:** Drip-brewed coffee at 87, 90, and 93 °C. Grind size and brew time were adjusted as needed to reach target TDS and percent extraction values.
- **Ingested takeaways (paraphrased):** Under those controlled drip-brew conditions, TDS and percent extraction affected sensory profile, while temperature had no appreciable independent effect once the target strength and extraction were held. This describes temperature effects after other variables were adjusted, not a claim that temperature never matters.
- **Correction checked:** Batali et al. (2021), “Author Correction…,” https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-021-82505-9. The correction changes a water-preparation instruction in the Methods from “per litre” to “per 100 litres.” It does not state a change to the reported sensory results.
- **Limits:** The primary result applies to drip brewing, 87–93 °C, the tested coffees and panel, and a design that adjusted grind/time to match TDS and PE. Do not generalize it to espresso, immersion, cold brewing, or uncontrolled temperature changes.
- **Use in this Wiki:** A case study for separating direct temperature effect from changes in extraction, and for checking whether a source has a linked correction.
