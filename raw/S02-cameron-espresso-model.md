# S02 — Systematically Improving Espresso

- **Citation:** Cameron et al. (2020), “Systematically Improving Espresso: Insights from Mathematical Modeling and Experiment,” *Matter*, 2(3), 631–648.
- **DOI / publisher record:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.matt.2019.12.019
- **Source type:** Peer-reviewed journal article combining mathematical modeling and experiment.
- **Access checked:** 2026-11-15. The publisher record/abstract and a university research record were checked. An institutional manuscript link exists, but the full manuscript was not reviewed in this ingest; this record paraphrases the published abstract and publisher highlights.
- **Scope:** Espresso extraction through a packed bed of coffee; the model examines grind setting, coffee dose, pressure, and extraction behavior.
- **Ingested takeaways (paraphrased):** The idealized homogeneous-flow model predicts one relationship between grind setting and extraction yield, but experiment found yield peaked at an intermediate setting, with lower yields at both coarse and fine extremes. The authors interpret this as evidence that flow becomes nonuniform at fine settings. Their results identify grinding and pressure as contributors to espresso reproducibility.
- **Limits:** This is an espresso study and its modeling assumptions matter. The reported pattern is not a universal rule for drip or immersion brewing, nor proof that every fine grind causes channeling.
- **Use in this Wiki:** Supports a method-specific explanation of why grind size affects both extraction and flow; use the observed non-monotonic result to avoid “finer is always better.”
