# S03 — Full-immersion strength and extraction-yield model

- **Citation:** Liang, J., Chan, K.C., & Ristenpart, W.D. (2021), “An equilibrium desorption model for the strength and extraction yield of full immersion brewed coffee,” *Scientific Reports*, 11, 6904.
- **DOI / publisher record:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-021-85787-1
- **Open full text:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7994670/
- **Source type / license:** Peer-reviewed primary research; open access, Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 (CC BY 4.0).
- **Access checked:** 2026-11-15. Publisher record and PMC full text/abstract checked.
- **Scope:** Predictive model and experiments on full-immersion brews, including coffee and water mass, brew ratio, temperature, grind size, roast, TDS, and equilibrium extraction yield.
- **Ingested takeaways (paraphrased):** At equilibrium, TDS was approximately inversely related to the water-to-coffee mass ratio, while extraction yield changed little across the tested brew ratios; the authors report values near 21% in the model/experimental range. Changing ratio can therefore change brew strength without producing the same change in extraction yield in these full-immersion conditions.
- **Limits:** The central claim concerns full immersion at or near equilibrium, not all short brews, percolation, or espresso. Equilibrium timing and measurement method matter; the paper discusses retained beverage in spent grounds as a source of bias in oven-dry estimates.
- **Use in this Wiki:** Defines the distinction between beverage concentration and extracted fraction, while demonstrating why a method-specific scope must accompany a numeric result.
