# S04 — Drip-brew strength, extraction, roast, and sensory profile

- **Citation:** Frost, S.C., Ristenpart, W.D., & Guinard, J.-X. (2020), “Effects of brew strength, brew yield, and roast on the sensory quality of drip brewed coffee,” *Journal of Food Science*, 85(8), 2530–2543.
- **DOI / PubMed record:** https://doi.org/10.1111/1750-3841.15326 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32652586/
- **Source type:** Peer-reviewed primary sensory study.
- **Access checked:** 2026-11-15. PubMed bibliographic record and abstract checked; journal full text was not reviewed in this ingest.
- **Scope:** A representative washed Arabica coffee at light, medium, and dark roasts. Drip brews were systematically varied by TDS (strength) and percent extraction (PE/EY); a trained descriptive analysis evaluated 30 sensory attributes.
- **Ingested takeaways (paraphrased):** Many sensory attributes varied with TDS and/or PE, and the relationships depended in part on roast level. TDS and PE therefore describe different dimensions of a brew but do not uniquely determine one simple flavor label. The authors report that sweetness had an inverse association with TDS across their tested roasts; other relationships were roast-dependent.
- **Limits:** The results concern drip brews, a single coffee, selected roast levels, and a descriptive panel. They do not establish universal preferences or a direct flavor prediction for every bean or brewing method.
- **Use in this Wiki:** Supports separating measurement from taste preference and avoiding a universal “ideal” extraction label.
